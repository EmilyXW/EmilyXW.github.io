Go Baby Go

What is Go Baby Go?

Go Baby Go is a national innitiative that provides customized toy cars for children with mobility challenges. Each car is modified specifically for its driver to empower them to explore and play with greater autonomy, with long-term positive developmental effects in areas such as motor function and cognition. The Go Baby Go event I participate in is co-organized by CAT and Ithaca College and is held annually. It culminates in a one-day engineering event where children and their families work alongside engineers to design and modify the cars to best fit their individual needs and preferences. This is an incredible experience as an engineer because I receive and implement feedback immediately, allowing me to participate in a rapid, iterative design cycle. I also have the opportunity to collaborate with professionals from Ithaca College's occupational and physical therapy departments, as well as the ultimate users of my cars. This direct collaboration makes the experience especially rewarding and has strengthened my understanding of user-centered engineering design.

My favorite things about Go Baby Go

I already touched on this in my previous paragraph, but my favorite thing about GoBabyGo is not only getting to use what I've learned through my engineering courses and projects make a positive impact on my community, but also getting to see that impact firsthand. The GoBabyGo events can run very long (my first one ran for 10 hours on the final car delivery day) and are in the middle of Cornell prelim season. Despite that, the people, passion, and care that go into the event make it something I'd never miss.

Everyone involved is kind, creative, and wonderful to work with, and it's always so fun doing rapid-fire design ideation and iteration with my team. Since we provide the cars completely free of charge, we have the additional challenge of working with cost-effective materials and adjusting our designs to an ever-changing inventory as multiple teams build cars simultaneously. It can be a long day, but it's worth it in ways that no competition or conference could match.

At the end of the day, we get to give the cars to the families and watch the children drive them for the first time. You should see the smiles on everyone's faces! The children drive around as the parents follow the cars around and take pictures; all the people who helped make the cars through stand by proudly and show off extra personalization features, like a disco ball or a music button in the child's favorite color.

I've always loved being able to make a positive impact in my community, from volunteering in high school to tutoring and leading courses at Cornell. I've also always been drawn to the intersection of engineering, medical research, and accessibility. GoBabyGo not only combines all of my interests but allows me to make a tangible positive impact on people. I highly recommend participating in the initiative if you’re able to. I can't express enough how incredible it is to see your creation make families smile and laugh and give children greater access to play and the world.
